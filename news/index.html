  • 26 Nov

    Bengaluru's izmo says it has built a 40 GHz RF chip package in India

    izmo Microsystems says it designed and made a QFN package for radio-frequency devices working up to 40 GHz, for an unnamed customer building modules for defence and secure wireless links. The company says the part has passed assembly and electrical checks and production shipments have begun. It lists radar and satellite communication among the target uses.

    Syllabus link: At tens of gigahertz, a package's bond wires and pads behave like circuit elements, so transmission lines and impedance matching from network theory and communication decide how well the chip performs.

    India · Source: Evertiq

  • 25 Nov

    TrendForce lowers its 2027 memory forecast and sees NAND flash prices falling

    The research firm says some Chinese cloud companies have cut back 2027 orders, and it now expects NAND to be oversupplied with falling contract prices next year. Even after the cuts, it expects revenue to grow 69% for DRAM, to $1.15 trillion, and 29% for NAND, to $419.3 billion. These are forecasts, not results.

    Industry · Source: TrendForce

  • 25 Nov

    SK hynix engineer targets 100-layer 3D DRAM in four to five years

    Speaking at a Seoul National University forum, SK hynix distinguished engineer Choi Kang-sik said mass production of 3D DRAM, which stacks memory cells vertically, could come around 2031. He said about 75 layers are needed just to match the density of today's 10 nm-class DRAM, and that existing equipment can manage 100 layers. Much taller stacks would need new tools.

    Syllabus link: A DRAM cell is one transistor and one capacitor that must be refreshed, a basic topic in digital electronics and solid state devices; 3D DRAM changes how that cell is laid out.

    Research · Source: The Elec

  • 24 Nov

    Chipmakers find AI struggles to reuse existing chip designs

    A Semiconductor Engineering report on industry talks says companies see mixed results when AI tools are used to create follow-on versions of a chip. The generated variants can differ a lot from the original and cost more than expected, and some of the data needed to prove a derivative works is held by foundries and packaging houses rather than the designer. Qualcomm's CTO reportedly said about three months went into tuning chip-design tool settings even though the logic and floorplan had not changed, while Siemens EDA said an agentic toolkit cut analysis of 112 high-speed channels from weeks to hours.

    Syllabus link: Reusing a block means re-checking its timing, power and layout in the new context, which is the practical side of VLSI design flow and verification.

    Chips and tools · Source: Semiconductor Engineering

  • 24 Nov

    Sponsor article cites a sharp fall in chips that work on first silicon

    A sponsored blog on Semiconductor Engineering, written by a verification-tool vendor's marketing manager, quotes the Wilson Research Group study as showing first-silicon success at about 5%, down from 14% in 2024 and 24% in 2022. It also says roughly half of projects need more than one re-spin, mostly because of design errors from changing specifications. These figures come second-hand through a vendor article and I have not checked the study itself, so treat them as unconfirmed.

    Syllabus link: A re-spin means fabricating a new mask set after a design bug, which is why functional verification is a core part of digital electronics and VLSI courses.

    Research · Source: Semiconductor Engineering (sponsor blog)

  • 23 Nov

    SCHMID launches a plating tool for copper-filled holes in glass chip substrates

    SCHMID Group introduced QuantumLine P+, equipment that fills tiny through-glass vias with copper from the bottom up, so no empty voids are left inside. The company says it handles holes up to 50 times deeper than they are wide, keeps thickness within about 6% across a panel, and runs four to five times faster than conventional plating. It is built for 510 x 515 mm panels with glass 200 to 1500 micrometres thick and can be ordered from December 2026. These are the maker's own claims. Glass cores are being explored for the large substrates that AI processors need.

    Chips and tools · Source: SCHMID via GlobeNewswire
