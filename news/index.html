Electronics industry news

The most important electronics and semiconductor stories, updated every day and explained in plain words. Each story links to its original source. Where a story connects naturally to a subject you study, that link is noted.

10 November 2026

  • 8 Nov

    Nvidia's board opens an independent review of its CEO after foreign-intelligence reports

    Nvidia's board said a committee of independent directors, helped by outside counsel, will look into recent reports that CEO Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. The board said it has no reason to believe company secrets or national security were compromised, and Huang said he supports the review and will cooperate fully. China's foreign ministry called the claims baseless.

    Industry · Source: CBS News

  • 8 Nov

    Diamond Foundry shows a 200 mm single-crystal diamond wafer for chip cooling

    Diamond Foundry says it has grown the first 200 mm single-crystal diamond wafer, the same size fabs use for older process nodes. Diamond conducts heat at least five times better than copper, so the aim is to pull heat out of hot AI chips. The sample shown is still unpolished, and the company thinks a 300 mm version may be possible before 2030.

    Research · Source: Tom's Hardware

  • 4 Nov

    India sets a 24-month target for a homegrown GPU and pushes SHAKTI and VEGA RISC-V chips

    MeitY Secretary S. Krishnan said India aims to develop an indigenous GPU in about two years under India Semiconductor Mission 2.0, which has an outlay of ₹1,27,500 crore. The government is also working to commercialise the SHAKTI and VEGA RISC-V processors as trusted chips for CCTV, IoT devices, drones and smart meters. No chip specifications or partners were named for the GPU.

    Syllabus link: RISC-V is an open instruction set architecture, so the processor design ideas you study in Digital Electronics and VLSI (pipelines, register files, control units) can be built without paying for an ISA licence.

    India · Source: Elets CIO

  • 9 Nov

    Vishay brings 800 V data-centre and EV power designs to electronica

    Ahead of electronica in Munich, Vishay listed its demos: a 1.6 kW converter using 650 V GaN MOSFETs to step a 400 V data-centre bus down to 50 V, a 12 kW 800 V HVDC converter, and a 40-phase voltage regulator for CPUs and GPUs. On the vehicle side it is showing a 22 kW bidirectional on-board charger built with SiC MOSFET modules. The list shows how fast AI racks and EVs are both moving to 800 V power.

    Chips and tools · Source: Vishay press release

9 November 2026

  • 5 Nov

    Microchip says AI buyers are taking over outside packaging lines, stretching lead times

    Microchip reported quarterly sales of $1.622 billion, up 42% from a year earlier, but guided the December quarter roughly flat because it cannot get enough capacity. Its CEO said AI companies have booked entire factories at outside assembly-and-test (OSAT) suppliers, and lead times for many standard parts have grown from the usual 4–8 weeks to 6–32 weeks. He also expects most foundries and OSATs to raise prices again around January.

    Industry · Source: 24/7 Wall St. via Yahoo Finance

  • 9 Nov

    Nvidia AI Day Seoul opens with Samsung and SK hynix showing GPU-accelerated chip simulation

    Nvidia's two-day event at COEX in Seoul, expected to draw about 2,000 people, focuses on AI agents, robotics and AI infrastructure. Samsung is presenting the use of Nvidia's PhysicsNeMo tool to speed up thermal, fluid and structural analysis, while SK hynix is using it with CUDA-X libraries to speed up semiconductor device and process simulation. LG is showing factory robots trained with Nvidia's GR00T model.

    Syllabus link: device and process simulation (TCAD) solves the same carrier-transport and doping equations you meet in Solid State Devices, just numerically and in 3D.

    Chips and tools · Source: BigGo Finance

  • 4 Nov

    Qualcomm's phone-chip sales fall 26% while automotive nearly doubles

    Qualcomm's revenue for its September quarter was $10.3 billion, down 8% from a year earlier. Handset chip revenue fell 26% to $5.1 billion, but automotive rose 89% to a record $2.0 billion and IoT grew 4%. The company kept its target of $5 billion in data-center revenue for fiscal 2027 and announced a multi-generation AI data-center collaboration with Amazon.

    Industry · Source: Qualcomm (SEC filing)

  • 2 Nov

    Marvell agrees to buy laser and optics maker Lumentum in a $115 billion stock deal

    Marvell will give 4.5 of its shares for each Lumentum share, valuing Lumentum at about $115 billion. Lumentum makes lasers, optical transceivers and optical circuit switches, and runs its own indium phosphide chip production. The companies argue that copper links are reaching their limits inside AI data centres; the deal needs shareholder and regulatory approval and is expected to close in the first half of 2028.

    Syllabus link: the lasers and photodetectors in optical transceivers are direct-bandgap compound semiconductors such as InP, which is why silicon alone cannot do this job.

    Industry · Source: Marvell and Lumentum press release (SEC filing)

8 November 2026

  • 6 Nov

    India opens applications for Semicon India 2.0 and clears 12 more component projects

    Electronics and IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw launched the web portal for the second phase of the Semicon India Programme, which has an outlay of ₹1,27,500 crore. Companies can now apply for three of its six pillars: equipment and materials, new fabs, and ATMP/OSAT (assembly and test) units; design, R&D and talent are not yet open. Separately, 12 more projects worth ₹7,503 crore were approved under the Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme, covering items such as multilayer PCBs, relays, copper foil and capacitor film, with about 7,127 direct jobs expected.

    India · Source: DD India

  • 7 Nov

    TSMC denies it is exploring 3D DRAM for Nvidia

    Responding to the report covered here yesterday, a TSMC spokesperson said the company stays focused on its core business as a dedicated logic foundry. Analysts note that 3D DRAM is still years from volume production even at Samsung, SK hynix and Micron, and is very hard to build without an existing planar DRAM process, which TSMC does not have.

    Chips and tools · Source: Tom's Hardware

  • 5 Nov

    Micron reportedly halts work on a Taiwan memory fab over a labour dispute

    Micron has reportedly all but stopped construction of Fab 11E in Taoyuan, a DRAM and HBM plant budgeted at up to $4.5 billion and planned to start in 2028. The local union is pushing for a larger share of AI-driven profits. Micron did not confirm the pause, saying only that its Taiwan operations continue as normal and talks with the union are ongoing.

    Industry · Source: The Edge Malaysia (Bloomberg)

  • 5 Nov

    EU launches a €525 million chip design platform for startups

    The European Chip Design Platform (EuroCDP), run by a consortium led by imec, gives smaller companies cloud access to EDA tools, IP blocks, design support and manufacturing runs. Early-stage startups can get grants of up to €100,000 for prototypes, and more mature firms up to €16 million. Europe currently earns less than 1% of global chip design revenue.

    Syllabus link: this is the VLSI design flow in practice: RTL, synthesis, place-and-route and verification all run on EDA tools, and the costly step for a startup is the first silicon (tape-out).

    Chips and tools · Source: AnySilicon

  • 2 Nov

    onsemi to buy Ferric to power AI processors

    onsemi agreed to acquire Ferric for $600 million in cash, plus up to $175 million more tied to revenue milestones. Ferric makes integrated voltage regulators that pack the controller, power FETs, inductors and capacitors into one small module placed right next to, or inside, the processor package, saving 50–70% of board area. The deal is expected to close by the end of 2026.

    Syllabus link: LIC covers linear voltage regulators; these are switching (buck) regulators, which trade the linear regulator's simplicity for much higher efficiency at large currents.

    Industry · Source: onsemi

7 November 2026

  • 3 Nov

    Global chip sales hit $517.7 billion in the third quarter

    The Semiconductor Industry Association reported worldwide chip sales of $517.7 billion for July–September 2026, up 24.1% from the previous quarter. September alone came to $172.6 billion, well over double September 2025, driven largely by demand for AI hardware.

    Industry · Source: Semiconductor Industry Association

  • 6 Nov

    Nvidia reportedly asks TSMC to make 3D DRAM

    According to reports, Nvidia has asked TSMC, the world's largest contract chipmaker, to manufacture advanced 3D DRAM, which stacks memory cells vertically. If TSMC agrees, it would compete with memory makers such as Samsung and Micron. Neither company has officially confirmed the plan.

    Syllabus link: a DRAM cell is one transistor plus one capacitor. The MOS capacitor in Solid State Devices (Module 3) explains how that charge is stored.

    Chips and tools · Source: NewsBytes

  • 5 Nov

    Intel's weak outlook and the memory shortage hit chip stocks

    Intel forecast fourth-quarter revenue of $15–16 billion, below analyst expectations. It blamed weaker PC demand and a shortage of memory chips that is raising costs, a shortage it expects to last at least into the first half of 2027. Chip stocks fell sharply that day amid wider worries about AI spending, then partly recovered on 6 November.

    Industry · Source: Yahoo Finance

  • 5 Nov

    US chip industry could be short of about 157,000 workers by 2030

    The industry group SEMI warns of a large gap in technicians and engineers as new US fabs come up. Community colleges are launching short programs: one in Arizona trains students in 12 weeks using VR and university cleanrooms. TSMC's Arizona project alone is expected to create around 25,000 jobs.

    Why it matters: fab process, device physics and test skills are in demand worldwide, including at India's new plants.

    Hiring · Source: KPBS

  • 3–4 Nov

    Delhi summit focuses on India's place in the chip supply chain

    The ISIG Executive Summit in New Delhi brought together the India Semiconductor Mission, the Semiconductor Laboratory (SCL) and companies including Infineon, Nexperia, Mitsubishi Electric and Navitas. Topics included advanced packaging, OSAT (assembly and test) and wide-bandgap power devices made from silicon carbide (SiC) and gallium nitride (GaN).

    India · Source: ANI
