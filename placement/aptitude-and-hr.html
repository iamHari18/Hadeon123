Most core electronics recruitments start with an online aptitude and technical test and end with an HR round. This page lists what the test covers and how to answer the HR questions freshers are asked most.

Aptitude test topics

Online tests for core roles usually have 3–4 sections in 60–90 minutes, often with sectional time limits and sometimes negative marking. Read the instructions on the test page for both before starting.

HR questions and how to answer them

HR rounds check communication, honesty and whether you will stay. For core companies the HR round often includes questions about your project and your interest in hardware, so it overlaps with the technical round.

H1Tell me about yourself.Easy

They want a 60–90 second summary that connects you to the role, not your life story. Structure: who you are now (final-year ECE student, college), what you are good at (two technical areas with evidence: a project, an internship, a result), and why this role. End with something they can ask about.

Weak: "I am from my hometown, my father is…, my hobbies are…". Better: "I'm a final-year ECE student at [your college]. Most of my work has been in embedded systems: my main project is a wireless sensor network for home automation, where I wrote the firmware for the nodes. I'm applying for this embedded role because I want to work on low-power devices."

H2Why do you want to join a core electronics company instead of IT?Easy

Give a reason backed by something you have done: a project you enjoyed, a lab you liked, hardware you built. Avoid running down IT jobs. "I enjoy work where I can measure the result on a board. During my mini project I spent two weeks debugging an SPI timing issue with a logic analyser and found I liked that kind of problem."

H3Why should we hire you?Easy

Match two or three of their requirements to evidence. Read the job description first and pick words from it. Keep it to three sentences and do not claim things you cannot show.

H4What are your strengths and weaknesses?Easy

Strength: one, with a short example. Weakness: a real one that does not disqualify you for the role, plus what you are doing about it. "I used to spend too long making a design perfect before testing it. I now build and test small pieces first; on my last project that caught a pin-mapping mistake on day one." Avoid "I am a perfectionist" with no example.

H5Explain your final-year project.Medium

This is the most important answer in both technical and HR rounds. Prepare a two-minute version: the problem, why it matters, your approach (block diagram you can draw), what you personally did, a number (accuracy, range, latency, power, cost), one problem you hit and how you solved it, and what you would improve. Be clear about which parts you did and which teammates did.

H6Describe a time you faced a conflict in a team.Medium

Use STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Choose a real, modest conflict (disagreement on a design choice or a deadline), show that you listened and used data to decide, and end with the result and what you learned. Do not blame a teammate.

H7Where do you see yourself in five years?Easy

Show that you plan to grow in the field the company works in. "I want to be the person my team trusts with a full subsystem, for example owning the firmware of a product from bring-up to production." If you plan higher studies, you do not need to mention it unless asked; never lie if asked directly.

H8Are you willing to relocate or work in shifts?Easy

Answer honestly. Many core roles are in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai, Pune or Noida, and test or manufacturing roles may involve shifts. If you have a constraint, say it now; it is worse to discover it after joining.

H9Do you have any questions for us?Medium

Always ask one or two. Good: "What would a fresher on this team work on in the first six months?", "Which tools and microcontrollers does the team use?", "How is training handled for new graduates?". Avoid asking about salary or leave in a technical or first HR round unless they raise it.

H10What do you know about our company?Medium

Look up, before the interview: what the company makes, its main products or customers, its locations in India, and what the role's team does. Mention one specific product. Saying "it is a leading MNC" tells them you did not look.

H11You have a bond or service agreement in the offer. Will you accept it?Hard

Read the terms before the interview if they are published. Ask clearly about duration, amount and what triggers it. If you accept, say so plainly; if you are unsure, say you would like to read the written terms. Do not agree verbally to something you have not seen.

H12Why is there a gap or a low score in your record?Medium

State the fact briefly without excuses, then say what changed. "My third-semester grades dropped while I was ill; from fourth semester my SGPA has been above 8." Interviewers mostly check whether you are honest and whether the issue has been resolved.

How to prepare

  1. Aptitude: 30–45 minutes daily for four weeks is enough for most tests. Do timed sets, not untimed reading; speed is the skill being tested.
  2. Keep a mistakes notebook: write each wrong question with the correct method. Revise it before every test.
  3. Mock the HR round with a friend: record your answer to "tell me about yourself" and "explain your project", and trim them until they fit the time.
  4. Prepare documents: updated résumé (one page for freshers), mark sheets, ID, and a short project write-up with a block diagram you can draw on paper.
  5. Do not memorise HR answers word for word. Prepare the points and the stories; the words should be your own on the day.
